Known Answers, Forced Structure, and the Limits of Transfer}
\author{Meadowlark Bradsher}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A rankability certificate based on the Hodge decomposition of a comparison graph
reports the fraction of comparison flow admitting no consistent scalar ordering.
Used as a decision procedure it needs a threshold, and the threshold cannot be
read off the mathematics: on finite, noisy, sparsely sampled data a genuinely
rankable criterion still deposits harmonic mass. This draft combines two lines of
work on that problem. The first manufactures comparison data whose decomposition
is known in closed form, validates an instrument against those known answers, and
records the estimator, guard, and reporting disciplines that survived contact
with measurement. The second asks which of those known answers are theorems about
the construction rather than empirical facts, and finds that the harmonic signal
is invariant across every bridge whose mean flow is a gradient, that the block
structure is forced by symmetry everywhere except on the bridge, and that one
bias-plus-variance identity on the harmonic projector organises the whole
apparatus. Their intersection is the operative result: the shape of every
harmonic reading is available without running the instrument, the constants are
not, and the reason is the same topology-dependence that forbids a universal
threshold.
\end{abstract}
